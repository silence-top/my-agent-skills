# Visual Directions

Use this reference when a request uses a broad aesthetic word or needs an initial art direction. Select one **primary direction** and, only if useful, one **modifier**. Translate the result into typography, palette, composition, material, and motion decisions before implementation.

| User wording | Direction | Translate into | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| 高级、精品、质感、Premium | Premium editorial | A restrained palette, deliberate type contrast, generous negative space, one or two carefully made materials, and precise motion. | Gold accents, blur, and oversized radius as shortcuts for luxury. |
| 简约、干净、克制 | Refined minimal | Fewer surfaces, strong hierarchy, purposeful whitespace, limited colors, and unusually careful alignment. | Empty pages, tiny low-contrast labels, or removing useful information. |
| 科技、未来、AI、酷 | Technical future | Structured grids, data-like details, high-contrast hierarchy, a controlled luminous accent, and crisp transitions. | Default dark background with cyan/purple gradients, glows everywhere, or decorative dashboards. |
| 温暖、治愈、亲和 | Warm human | Soft but readable neutrals, tactile imagery or illustration, friendly type, rounded-but-not-uniform forms, and gentle transitions. | Excessive pastels, low contrast, or making every element pill-shaped. |
| 年轻、活力、潮流 | Bold graphic | Strong scale changes, energetic color blocking, expressive type, cropped imagery, and confident composition. | Random stickers, too many colors, or motion that harms task completion. |
| 专业、企业、可信赖 | Calm institutional | Clear navigation, sober colors, dense-but-legible information, consistent components, and direct copy. | Generic SaaS card grids, sterile white space, or visual decoration that competes with data. |
| 杂志、艺术、故事感 | Editorial | Dominant type, asymmetric composition, image-text rhythm, intentional cropping, and a limited but opinionated palette. | Making normal forms and dense tools hard to scan for the sake of art direction. |
| 自然、有机、手作 | Organic | Earth-derived color, textured or photographic accents where relevant, softer geometry, and an unhurried rhythm. | Fake paper texture everywhere or sacrificing clarity for handmade effects. |
| 复古、怀旧 | Contemporary retro | Choose a specific era cue, pair it with modern accessibility and layout, and use period-appropriate type or color sparingly. | Mixing several decades or recreating obsolete interaction patterns. |
| 极繁、海报感、视觉冲击 | Maximalist composition | Layering, contrast, scale, and a deliberate visual focal point; reserve calm zones for reading and action. | Uniform noise, unreadable text, or animation on every element. |
| 粗野、硬朗、Brutalist | Raw structural | Visible grid logic, strong borders, direct typography, and intentionally plain materials. | Accidental ugliness, poor spacing, or inaccessible contrast. |
| 玻璃、空间感、Liquid Glass | Layered translucent material | A defined material hierarchy, backdrop contrast, edge highlights, depth, and state-aware motion. | One translucent card with blur declared as the complete style. |

## Modifiers

Use a modifier only when it strengthens the primary direction:

- **Dense / information-rich:** reduce padding waste; add scan-friendly grouping and hierarchy.
- **Cinematic:** use one immersive visual moment and restrained supporting UI.
- **Playful:** add expressive micro-interactions and friendly copy without obscuring system status.
- **Native:** favor platform components, gestures, focus behavior, and system typography over brand novelty.
- **Quiet:** reduce color and motion while retaining clear hierarchy and useful content density.

## Direction output

Before building a significant new surface, state the selected direction in one compact sentence: primary direction, optional modifier, visual focal point, and the one or two elements deliberately not being used. Example: “Premium editorial with a quiet modifier: oversized serif feature title and editorial photography; no glass panels or gradient background.”

## Visual quality check

Before calling a significant new surface done, inspect a rendered view and ask:

- Is the first visual focal point obvious, and does it reinforce the page's main task?
- Is there a clear supporting hierarchy, rather than every section and card having equal weight?
- Does the content density suit the product and screen, or is useful space left empty without purpose?
- Is the chosen visual direction visible in the actual typography, imagery, composition, color, material, or motion—not only named in the plan?
- Are decorative elements earning their space, and does the page still feel coherent without relying on default cards, gradients, and shadows?

If the page feels too plain, strengthen the focal point or hierarchy first. Add decoration only when it communicates product identity, content, state, or interaction.
